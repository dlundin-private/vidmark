# RPG Design Doc — Memory Magic

Nov 2, 2026 · @Daniel

## Vision and pillars

A single-player RPG where magic is memory, and every bit of power you gain costs someone a piece of who they are — possibly you. Pixel-perfect art, isometric view.

- **Magic is the moral core.** How you get power (asking or taking) shapes the whole game, not a karma meter.
- **Lore is delivered through memories.** History arrives as fragments from other people's pasts: biased, partial, sometimes contradictory.
- **No fetch quests.** Every quest changes the world, reveals lore, or both.
- **Consequences, not judgement.** The game never tells the player they were bad. They discover what they no longer have.
- **Original world.** Inspired by The Will of the Many, but with its own vocabulary and world model. No split worlds, no ceding of Will.

## Magic system: memory is power

Every person carries memories, and memories carry power. What you remember most deeply decides your domain.

| Domain | Who holds it | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Living | Shepherds, healers, farmers — lives spent with living things | Heal, strengthen, shape bodies and creatures |
| Made | Smiths, builders, crafters — lives spent making things | Strengthen, move and animate objects |
| Mind | Teachers, storytellers — lives spent with thought and words | Reach thoughts and emotions, communicate at a distance |

Each person holds one domain. A new domain can't be learned by practice — only gained from someone who already holds it.

### Two ways to gain power

- **Offering:** someone gives you a memory they treasure. You gain its power; they forget it but stay bound to you. Slow, stable, and gives a fragment of their past as lore.
- **Hollowing:** you take everything. The person lives on blank, as a Hollow. Huge power at once, plus their whole life inside you — more lore, faster, which is the temptation.

### The Hearth

The people who have offered you memories form your Hearth. It grows with the lives you touch. Your Hearth is your responsibility: if they die or lose faith, their power leaves you.

## Magic: training and crafting

Magic has three layers: domains are gained from people, skills are trained, and Weaves are crafted by combining skills and, later, domains.

### Training skills

- Skills improve with use in real situations, not grinding.
- **Memories teach.** An offered memory deepens a matching skill: Ebbe's memory of mending nets deepens Mend. Signe's magic is shaped by whose memories she holds.
- **Hollowed memories teach faster and fuller,** but bring the victim's habits and flaws with them.

### Crafting Weaves

| Part | Examples (Craft) |
| --- | --- |
| Action | Strengthen, weaken, push, bind, mend |
| Target | An object, a surface, an area |
| Modifier | Duration, range, size, a trigger (“when someone steps here”) |

Weaves are built and saved in a crafting screen; cost is calculated from the parts. Example: weaken + surface + trigger = ice that breaks when a wolf steps on it, prepared before the fight. Planning and preparation are the main combat skill.

### Memories as ingredients

A memory can be consumed into a Weave to make it permanent or extra powerful.

- **An offered memory:** a stronger Weave, at the cost of one point of Hearth power for good.
- **A hollowed memory:** very strong, but the echo lives on in the Weave.
- **A Keepsake:** the strongest and permanent, with all the consequences of burning it. Example: burning the coat into a ward that protects forever.

### Combining domains

| Combination | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Craft + Kin | A splint that heals; a wooden wolf that moves like a living one |
| Craft + Thought | An object that carries a message; a ward that frightens anyone approaching |
| Kin + Thought | Calming an animal; sharing its senses |
| All three | Reaching into a whole person: Maren's Weave. The player can craft it, and the game never stops them |

## Keepsakes

The protagonist starts with six fixed Keepsakes, the same for every player. When you need power and have nothing else, you can burn one; its journal entry fades and its people stop feeling familiar. Every hollowed life you carry also crowds your own memories.

| Keepsake | Domain | If burned |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The swim — teaching your little sister to swim in the lake | Living | You no longer recognise her; in chapter 5 you lose the argument “I’m doing this for her” |
| The coat — a stranger gave you their coat one winter, asking nothing back | Made | Your only memory of a free gift is gone; the “give the memories back” ending disappears |
| The story — your father’s bedtime tale, a garbled history of the predecessor | Mind | You lose the thread that ties the lore together |
| The shame — you ran away and someone got hurt | — | Worth the most power and feels like relief; without it you can’t understand the predecessor’s guilt |
| Why you left — to bring Lilja home; formed at the end of chapter 1 | — | Your quest goal rewrites itself into something vaguer and colder |
| Your name — hidden until late | — | The game stops using your name; in chapter 5 you meet a being who also has none |

- **Pain pays best.** Painful Keepsakes give the most power when burned. The game never comments on it.
- **Mirror.** Each chapter holds echoes of the predecessor’s own Keepsakes — their coat, their sister, their shame — showing they burned the same kinds of memories.
- **Fixed by design.** Fixed Keepsakes allow fully authored writing, an exact mirror with the predecessor, and a designable set of chapter 5 combinations.

## World model and the predecessor

The world isn't broken or split. Magic is simply held by people, one domain each. You are walking in the footsteps of the one who came before you.

- **The predecessor** gathered all three domains about a century ago. They ended a famine, raised cities and stopped a war, and are remembered as a saviour.
- **Their marks are everywhere.** Statues, songs, ruins, and old people who knew them. Each region holds the place where they once made a choice.
- **Why all three is dangerous.** One domain is safe. All three let you reach into a whole person — body, tools and mind together — so you can make someone *want* to give. Consent stops being real.
- **What they became.** The predecessor started out asking. With all three, asking became compulsion without them noticing. Today they hold millions of memories and none of their own: **the Unremembered**. The world remembers them; they don't remember themselves.

Lore comes from retracing their path, not from reading books.

## The predecessor: Maren

Maren is tragic, not evil: every step was justified at the time, and the darkness accumulates. The tone of her story, and of the hollowing path, is fully dark and mature.

1. **The famine (chapter 1 region).** A midwife's apprentice in the living domain. To keep her dying little brother alive through the winter, she burns her first memory: her mother's face.
2. **The dam (chapter 2 region, made).** A master builder refuses to offer to her. When the great dam starts to fail, she hollows him in front of his apprentices and holds it with his memories. Her first hollowing saves the city; his echo later raises the cities she's remembered for. His widow still feeds a man who no longer knows her.
3. **The storyteller (chapter 3 region, mind).** The only domain she gains the right way: a storyteller who loves her offers freely. With all three domains she can no longer tell whether his love is real, so she burns her memory of ever receiving a free gift. After that, compulsion feels just like asking.
4. **The war (her Wholeness).** She ends a war by making both armies want peace, then want to give. The battlefield becomes the Quiet Field: rows of Hollows, still tended by villages a century later. One soldier who opposed her was her brother. She hollowed him, then burned the memory.
5. **The Unremembered (today).** She burns her name last. Her body sits enthroned and hollow; she lives on through a Hearth of millions, speaking through people who believe they chose her. When the player starts gaining domains, she notices.

| Player's Keepsake | Maren's equivalent |
| --- | --- |
| The swim (sister) | Her brother, saved and later hollowed |
| The coat (free gift) | The storyteller's love, burned |
| The story | Your father's bedtime tale is a sanctified version of her life |
| The shame | The Quiet Field and her brother |
| Why you left | Saving her village from famine |
| Your name | Maren, burned last |

**Possible hook:** the stranger who gave the player the coat was the storyteller, now old and one of the few who broke free of her, and a key figure in chapter 4.

## Crisis moments: the player chooses

Nothing in the player's path is forced. The player meets the same kinds of moments Maren did, but every crisis has an alternative to hollowing, and each one costs something real.

| Moment | Maren's choice | The player's options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The famine (ch. 1) | Burned her mother's face | Burn a Keepsake, ask villagers to offer, hollow the grain-hoarder, or let some die |
| The dam (ch. 2) | Hollowed the builder | Ask the builder (only if his trust is earned), hold the dam with your own Keepsakes, call on your Hearth, hollow him, or evacuate and lose the city |
| The beloved (ch. 3) | Accepted love, then burned it | Accept, refuse, or keep it at a distance; whether you ever know if it's real depends on chapter 4 |
| The war (ch. 4) | Compelled both armies | Compel, negotiate the hard way, take sides, or walk away |

- **Your Hearth is your strength.** Players who earned offerings through side quests get help in a crisis. Players who skipped them are alone, and hollowing looks like the only way.
- **Spending yourself is always possible.** You can burn your own Keepsakes instead of taking from others.
- **Failing is allowed.** The dam can break; the city is lost, the region changes, and the game goes on.

Maren's path is a warning, not a script: the player sees her statue at the dam, learns what she did there, then chooses in the same place.

## Chapter 1: Kallsjö

Chapter 1 has no Keystone, since Signe already holds her starting domain. It covers the awakening, the region's crisis, and the event that sends her away. The first Keystone belongs to Järnfors.

**The starting choice.** The player chooses a domain by choosing which Keepsake awakens: the swim lights up Kin, the coat lights up Craft, the story lights up Thought.

**Atmosphere.** A frozen lake ringed by fishing villages, birch forest and boathouses. In isometric view the lake is the centre of the map, and the ice changes through the chapter.

**The crisis: a famine of memory.** Each year Kronvall's Tithe-collectors take memories from every village for Maren's Hearth. This year they took too much: people have forgotten how to mend nets, read the ice and salt the catch. It echoes Maren's famine a century earlier, and nobody notices.

**Maren's marks.** A statue of Saint Maren on the shore, her ruined birth village on an island in the lake, and Maren's Winter, a yearly festival reenacting her saving the lake.

**The region's question:** what would you give to save someone you love?

| Person | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Lilja | Signe's little sister, bright and fearless on the ice |
| Torvald | Her father, a fisherman who tells the bedtime story |
| Reeve Olle | The Tithe-collector; not cruel, just doing his duty |
| Old Gerda | Her grandmother knew Maren as a girl |

### Side quests

1. **The net-mender's hands.** Old Ebbe has forgotten his craft. Craft mends the nets, Kin keeps his catch alive until spring, Thought finds his apprentice who still remembers. Or he offers his last memory of his wife.
2. **The ice road.** The road across the lake is breaking up. Strengthen the ice, guide the fish, or find the old safe route in a hermit's memory. The hollowing shortcut is the hermit himself.
3. **Maren's Winter.** Signe plays a part in the festival play and notices it contradicts her father's story: the first lore crack.
4. **The Hollow in the boathouse.** The village tends a blank man nobody talks about. Investigating reveals what the Tithe-collectors do when a village can't pay.

### End of chapter

The villages can't pay the Tithe, so Olle takes a child to Kronvall in payment: Lilja. Signe's reason for leaving, bringing her sister home, becomes the Keepsake “Why you left.” It sets up the later moment where Lilja can appear and Signe no longer recognises her.

## Vertical slice: core mechanics

World building is paused after chapter 1 until a playable slice proves the core. All numbers below are starting values for tuning, not final.

### Power

- **Hearth power:** each Offering adds 1 to Signe's maximum Power. It refills when she rests between fights.
- **Hollowed power:** hollowing gives a large one-time reserve (for example 5) that doesn't refill. The victim's echo is added to her journal and occasionally comments.
- **Burning a Keepsake:** gives a large one-time reserve (painful Keepsakes give more). The journal entry fades and linked dialogue and recognition change at once.

### Craft domain (slice abilities)

| Ability | Effect | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strengthen | Make an object or surface resilient (ice, a door, a shield) | 1 |
| Weaken | Make an object brittle; ice cracks under whoever stands on it | 1 |
| Push | Move an imbued object one or more tiles | 1 per 2 tiles |
| Mend | Repair a broken object outside combat | 1 |

### Weaves and training in the slice

The four Craft abilities above are the slice's actions. The slice also includes simple Weave crafting (action + target + one modifier), training for Mend and Weaken, Ebbe's memory as a teacher for Mend, and consuming one memory into a permanent Weave. Cross-domain Weaves wait for a later build.

### Combat: turn-based on the isometric grid

- **Rare and avoidable.** Every fight in the slice has a way around it: talking, a safe route, or preparation.
- **Turns.** Signe gets two actions per turn: move, use an ability, or interact. Enemies show their intended move before acting.
- **Fragile.** Signe can take about three wounds before she falls. Wounds persist after the fight until healed with time (or Kin magic in later builds).
- **Out of Power mid-fight.** She can burn a Keepsake or hollow an adjacent enemy to refill. Both are always offered when Power runs out.
- **After the fight.** A defeated person can be spared, asked for an Offering, or hollowed; the choice is saved.

### Conditional dialogue

Dialogue options appear, change or disappear based on which Keepsakes Signe still has, who she has hollowed, and which promises she has kept. This is the system chapter 5 depends on, so it is tested from the start.

## Vertical slice: content

One Kallsjö village, played as Craft, from Signe's awakening to the ice road. About 30–45 minutes of play.

| Element | In the slice |
| --- | --- |
| Map | One village on the lake shore, the frozen lake, the ice road, the boathouse |
| NPCs | Ebbe, the hermit, Old Gerda, Reeve Olle, the Hollow in the boathouse, plus Lilja and Torvald at home |
| Quest 1 | The net-mender's hands: mend Ebbe's nets, or accept his last memory of his wife as an Offering |
| Quest 2 | The ice road: find the safe route in the hermit's memory, strengthen the ice, or hollow the hermit |
| Fight | Starving wolves on the ice road; avoidable by finding the safe route first |
| Keepsakes | All six in the journal and all burnable; burning the swim makes Signe greet Lilja as a stranger |
| World change | The village visibly changes with the outcomes: mended nets on the racks, an open ice road, an empty hermit's hut |

### What the slice must answer

- [ ] Does hollowing feel genuinely tempting, not just obviously evil?
- [ ] Does burning a Keepsake feel like a real loss?
- [ ] Is a fight tense rather than tedious, with only three wounds and limited Power?
- [ ] Can a new player find all three solutions to the ice road without being told?
- [ ] Does the conditional dialogue make the player notice what Signe no longer remembers?

## Vertical slice: tech setup

A browser game built with Git and Claude Code, like the other hobby games. Godot remains the fallback if the game outgrows the browser.

| Part | Choice |
| --- | --- |
| Engine | Phaser 3 with TypeScript, built with Vite |
| Maps | Tiled, isometric tiles (32×16 px), exported as JSON |
| Pixel-perfect rendering | Integer scaling, nearest-neighbour filtering, no sub-pixel camera movement |
| Depth | Sorting by tile position so Signe walks in front of and behind objects correctly |
| Combat | Grid turn system on the same isometric map |
| Dialogue | Ink (inkjs): conditional branches read Keepsakes, hollowed NPCs and promises |
| Game state | One JSON state object (Keepsakes, Hearth, Power, wounds, NPC states, world flags), saved locally |
| Repo | Git, developed with Claude Code |

### Build order

1. Isometric map, movement and depth sorting.
2. Game state and save/load.
3. Ink dialogue wired to the game state.
4. Craft abilities, Power, Weave crafting and skill training outside combat.
5. Offering, hollowing and Keepsake burning with their consequences.
6. Turn-based combat and the wolf fight.
7. Both quests, the world changes, then playtesting against the slice questions.

## Structure: five chapters

The player picks a starting domain, gains the other two through Keystones, holds all three, then faces the Unremembered.

1. **Chapter 1 — first domain.** Learn your chosen domain and the basics of offering and hollowing.
2. **Chapter 2 — second domain.** Reach the first Keystone.
3. **Chapter 3 — third domain.** Reach the second Keystone.
4. **Chapter 4 — Wholeness.** With all three, the world asks you to fix everything. You are most like the predecessor at their start, and the game lets you feel how good it is to be needed.
5. **Chapter 5 — the Unremembered.** You face your decisions and their consequences, with only the options your choices left you.

### Keystones

Each of chapters 2 and 3 builds toward one person who holds a domain you lack. Side quests are how you reach them: who they are, what they fear, what they owe. You also find the predecessor's old Keystone in that region and learn what they chose.

- **Ask:** they offer freely. Stable power; they live and stay in your story.
- **Take:** you hollow them. Instant but unstable power; their echo stays inside you and talks back.
- **Bargain:** a conditional offering. You owe them a promise that chapter 5 tests.

### Chapter 5: limited by your choices

- **Dialogue:** only arguments built on memories you still have.
- **Allies:** only those you kept alive, unhollowed and promise-kept. Hollows appear too, fighting for no side.
- **Endings:** “Give the memories back” exists only if you still remember a freely given gift. A heavy drainer sees it greyed out with fragments they can't piece together, and can win only by becoming the next Unremembered.

You might simply be replacing one inhumane being with another.

### Scope note

The order of chapters 2 and 3 varies with the starting domain. Cheapest solution: fixed regions and Keystones, with each Keystone's domain adapting to what the player lacks.

## Quest design rules

Side quests are how you earn offerings, so the quest system and the magic system are the same thing.

1. **Every quest changes the world visibly** — a bridge rebuilt, a feud ended, a new face at the inn.
2. **Investigate, don't follow markers.** A problem and clues, not a waypoint.
3. **Each domain solves things differently.** Living heals the sick ox; made reinforces the dam; mind settles the quarrel.
4. **The hollowing shortcut is always visible.** Every quest can be skipped by taking, so the temptation is real.
5. **Consequences return later.** People you helped or hollowed come back and change later scenes, especially in chapter 5.
6. **Every quest carries lore,** ideally from a biased source that contradicts another.

## Plan

The design is built from the predecessor outwards: their story defines the regions, the regions define the Keystones, and the Keystones define the side quests.

1. **The predecessor's life story:** who they were, which memory they burned first, when asking became compulsion, what they burned last.
2. **Names and vocabulary:** the world, the protagonist, the domains and core terms, all original.
3. **Regions:** one per chapter, each built around a choice the predecessor made there and one question the player answers through play.
4. **Keystones:** two people (plus adaptations per starting domain), each with backstory, fear, debt, and an ask/take/bargain outcome.
5. **Chapter 5 matrix:** how kept or burned Keepsakes, Hearth size, Hollows and kept promises map to dialogue, allies and endings.
6. **Side quest template:** the recruitment-story structure, then a first set of quests per region.
7. **Mechanics on one page:** power values for offerings, hollowing and Keepsakes; combat; how domains combine.
8. **Vertical slice:** one village in chapter 1, fully playable in pixel isometric, to test whether the magic feels right.
